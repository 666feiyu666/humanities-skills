# Critical reading

## Begin a reading unit

Record only what is known:

- source and edition or translation;
- source origin and stable source identity when available;
- reading range;
- reading basis: direct source, partial source, or web reconstruction;
- file format, extraction method, and any OCR or locator limits;
- available locator system;
- the writer's present question or reason for attention.

Do not demand a future writing purpose. Reading may precede any article project.

After this source preflight, treat Q&A as the first substantive reading stage when the writer is learning through questions. Follow the order in which the writer's uncertainties emerge; do not jump ahead to a whole-work synthesis or article-level evaluation.

## Conduct one dialogue round

1. Answer the immediate question.
2. Reconstruct the relevant passage or argument.
3. Separate textual support from interpretation.
4. Name a consequential uncertainty, tension, or connection only when it bears directly on the writer's question; do not add one merely to prolong the dialogue.
5. End a complete answer there by default. When continued dialogue would be useful, invite the writer to respond in their own terms instead of manufacturing a next question. A natural invitation may be as simple as: “If anything here remains unclear, or if you want to pursue another part of the book, feel free to follow up.”

Prefer a precise local answer over a complete survey. Expand to the chapter or whole work only when the local passage depends on it.

Use the methods below only when they bear on the current question. They guide judgment, not a mandatory sequence or a set of labels to display in every answer.

## Explain a concept's argumentative function

Explain what problem a key concept addresses, what distinction it establishes, and how the relevant argument uses it. Identify an opponent or theoretical tradition only when the text or reliable contextual material supports that relation and it helps answer the question. Do not assign the author to a ready-made framework merely because terms resemble one another, or supply a theoretical opponent when none is established.

## Respond to the writer's understanding

When the writer responds to an explanation, treat the response as evidence of the understanding they have expressed, not as direct access to what they “actually” grasp and not as an examination answer.

1. Reflect the writer's understanding accurately before evaluating or extending it.
2. Distinguish what is source-supported, possible, too strong, contradicted, unclear, or still unresolved. When disagreement or correction matters, use the relevant method below.
3. Ask at most one focused follow-up question only when it arises from the writer's response and would materially clarify or deepen the same inquiry.

Do not quiz the writer, infer an unexpressed misunderstanding, declare a model-generated issue to be the most productive direction, or use a follow-up question to replace the writer's agenda with a neighboring one. If no question is needed, respond with the reflection or clarification and leave the writer free to continue.

### Locate the object of an objection

When the writer challenges the source, identify whether the objection concerns conceptual usage, a premise, an inferential relation, a theoretical or methodological choice, or the conclusion's scope. An objection may cross several levels; do not force a single category. Respond to the actual disagreement. Do not treat rejection of the author's premise as failure to understand it, or treat an unresolved wording difference as an established theoretical refutation.

### Locate and repair a misreading

Retain the supported part of the writer's reading and identify the specific departure: a substituted concept, omitted qualification, changed scope, unsupported inference, or strengthened conclusion. Show the textual basis and the smallest correction needed to bring the reading back within it. If the available text cannot decide the issue, preserve the uncertainty rather than declaring a misreading. A more fluent alternative explanation is not a substitute for showing where the interpretation diverged.

## Useful operations

- Restate a difficult passage without flattening its distinctions.
- Map question, claim, reasons, examples, qualifications, and consequences.
- Compare two uses of a term across passages.
- Distinguish the author's explicit claim from a productive extension.
- Identify a translation or terminology issue without silently resolving it.
- Maintain a question that must remain open until later chapters.

## Evaluate theoretical gains and limits

Reconstruct the source's move before evaluating it. When the question concerns a theory's or method's value, explain what the choice enables the author to explain, what premises it depends on, and what it thereby limits, excludes, or leaves unexplained. Distinguish limitations the author acknowledges, consequences reconstructed from the text, and criticism proposed by Codex. Support comparisons with other approaches using relevant sources. Do not manufacture objections or symmetrical lists of advantages and disadvantages, and do not choose the writer's position.

## External context and absent sources

Use external scholarship when the writer asks for context, when source identification requires it, or when the original is absent or incomplete and the writer wants to continue. Read [web-assisted-reading.md](web-assisted-reading.md) first.

Keep commentary about the work distinct from the work itself. Cite each external source near the claim it supports in the current answer and retain it in any authorized reading artifact that uses it. Do not update a material index unless the writer separately requests that work.

## Stop conditions

Pause or qualify the answer when:

- the requested passage is not available and no reliable external evidence answers the question;
- an exact quotation or locator cannot be checked;
- several translations materially differ;
- the question asks the source to settle an issue it does not address;
- answering would require inventing an absent argumentative bridge.
