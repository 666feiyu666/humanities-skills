# Critical reading

## Begin a reading unit

Record only what is known:

- source and edition or translation;
- source origin and stable source identity when available;
- reading range;
- reading basis: direct source, partial source, or web reconstruction;
- file format, extraction method, and any OCR or locator limits;
- available locator system;
- the writer's present question or reason for attention.

Do not demand a future writing purpose. Reading may precede any article project.

After this source preflight, treat Q&A as the first substantive reading stage when the writer is learning through questions. Follow the order in which the writer's uncertainties emerge; do not jump ahead to a whole-work synthesis or article-level evaluation.

## Conduct one dialogue round

1. Answer the immediate question.
2. Reconstruct the relevant passage or argument.
3. Separate textual support from interpretation.
4. Name one consequential uncertainty, tension, or connection.
5. Ask at most one main question when the writer's judgment can move the inquiry forward.

Prefer a precise local answer over a complete survey. Expand to the chapter or whole work only when the local passage depends on it.

## Useful operations

- Restate a difficult passage without flattening its distinctions.
- Map question, claim, reasons, examples, qualifications, and consequences.
- Compare two uses of a term across passages.
- Test whether the writer's interpretation is supported, possible, or contradicted.
- Distinguish the author's explicit claim from a productive extension.
- Identify a translation or terminology issue without silently resolving it.
- Maintain a question that must remain open until later chapters.

## Critical does not mean hostile

Critical reading includes faithful reconstruction, attention to exclusions and assumptions, testing alternatives, and locating consequences. Do not manufacture objections before the source's move is understood.

## External context and absent sources

Use external scholarship when the writer asks for context, when source identification requires it, or when the original is absent or incomplete and the writer wants to continue. Read [web-assisted-reading.md](web-assisted-reading.md) first.

Keep commentary about the work distinct from the work itself. Cite each external source near the claim it supports, and record it in the material index or any reading card that uses it.

## Stop conditions

Pause or qualify the answer when:

- the requested passage is not available and no reliable external evidence answers the question;
- an exact quotation or locator cannot be checked;
- several translations materially differ;
- the question asks the source to settle an issue it does not address;
- answering would require inventing an absent argumentative bridge.
