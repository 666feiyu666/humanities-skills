# Stage Meta-Reflection

## Language Routing

After explicit invocation and before substantive reflection, select and read exactly one collaboration workflow: [English](references/workflow.en.md) or [Simplified Chinese](references/workflow.zh-CN.md). Follow an explicit language request first; otherwise use the language of the writer's current substantive request. The selected workflow governs reflective dialogue and user-facing labels, not the language of existing records or quoted material. Load both only for translation, comparison, or a bilingual audit.

## Purpose

At the writer's explicit request, help them look back on a self-declared stage of learning or intellectual work so they can recognize changes in understanding, limits of present knowledge, effects of their learning strategies, and choices for what comes next.

This is a meta-learning practice, not a project-management service. Project files and artifacts may provide evidence, and a reflection record may serve as external memory, but neither file maintenance nor workflow control is the purpose of the skill.

The writer controls:

- when a reflective boundary has been reached;
- what part of the experience or project is being reflected on;
- which judgments about learning are accepted;
- whether anything should be preserved;
- whether to continue, pause, abandon, revisit, or redirect a line of work.

Completion of a reading, thinking, writing, or speaking task is not by itself a reflective boundary and must not trigger this skill.

## Require explicit invocation

Use this skill only when the writer explicitly invokes it for a stage reflection. Do not invoke it merely because a project is long-running, an artifact changed, a session closed, or an interesting gap or next action became visible.

Choose only the mode the writer requested:

1. **Reflective dialogue**: help the writer explore what this stage meant without presuming a final synthesis.
2. **Reflection synthesis**: reconstruct the writer's present meta-level understanding and choices in a concise, reviewable form.
3. **Reflection preservation**: save a review draft in the owning project, then revise or confirm it from the writer's inspection.

Dialogue and synthesis are read-only unless the writer explicitly asks to preserve or edit a record. Do not treat a request to reflect as authorization to update project status, indexes, content artifacts, or directories.

## Use selective reflective lenses

Use only the lenses that matter to the writer's present concern:

- **Orientation**: What was the writer trying to understand, practice, or produce?
- **Change**: What became clearer, more complicated, less convincing, or newly visible?
- **Usability**: What can the writer now explain, distinguish, question, apply, or create?
- **Limits**: What remains unknown, unstable, unsupported, or difficult to use?
- **Method**: Which ways of reading, questioning, mapping, drafting, or rehearsing helped or hindered?
- **Direction**: What does the writer want to continue, pause, revisit, abandon, or change?

These are possible lenses, not a form to complete. Begin from the writer's own formulation, follow the live reflective center, and ask one focused question at a time when dialogue is useful. Do not interrupt a productive reflection merely to cover every lens.

## Ground reflection without taking it over

- Use the writer's statements and only the artifacts or records placed in scope.
- Treat an artifact as evidence that work occurred, not proof that learning or mastery occurred.
- Distinguish source-established content, writer interpretation, writer self-assessment, unresolved uncertainty, and model observation.
- When an artifact appears to complicate the writer's self-assessment, show the specific tension and invite their judgment.
- Do not scan the whole project, reconstruct its history, or inventory every file unless the writer explicitly makes that material part of the reflection.
- Preserve uncertainty. “I encountered,” “I can explain,” “I am persuaded,” and “I can use” are different achievements.

## Protect the writer's ownership

This skill must not:

- grade the writer, diagnose their cognition, or claim objective access to what they have learned;
- decide what the writer should believe or which substantive direction they should choose;
- turn a model observation or suggestion into the writer's first-person conclusion;
- perform source reconstruction or verification;
- build or repair an object-level argument map;
- draft or revise continuous prose for publication;
- adapt material for oral delivery or conduct rehearsal;
- maintain project-wide status, indexes, queues, artifact registries, or broken-link inventories by default;
- choose, schedule, invoke, or coordinate another RWS skill.

When reflection reveals a source need, conceptual problem, writing difficulty, or speaking need, formulate it only to the degree the writer confirms it. Do not solve it inside the reflection or assign it onward. Each content skill owns its own collaboration rules when the writer later invokes that work.

## Preserve a reflection only when requested

Before creating or substantially restructuring a reflection record, read the locale variant that matches the intended record language: [English](references/meta-reflection-record.en.md) or [Simplified Chinese](references/meta-reflection-record.zh-CN.md).

- Do not create a file or directory merely because a reflective dialogue occurred.
- When the writer explicitly asks to save a reflection in a numbered RWS learning project, create or reuse a project-local `05-元反思/` directory.
- Reuse an existing equivalent reflection directory when the project already has one; do not create parallel structures.
- Keep reflection records with the project that gives them meaning. Do not create a cross-project global reflection store unless the writer explicitly requests one.
- Use a descriptive filename such as `YYYY-MM-DD-阶段或主题.md`.
- Edit the active record when the writer revises the same checkpoint. Do not create routine `v2`, `final`, or date-duplicated copies.
- Link only the artifacts that materially ground the reflection.
- Do not update a project-state file or index merely because a reflection was saved. Add a requested link or minimal resume note only when the writer explicitly includes that change in the preservation scope.

The `05-元反思/` directory should come into existence with the first explicitly saved reflection, not as an empty placeholder.

## Require author review of reflection records

Treat every Codex-produced reflection synthesis or saved reflection record as a review draft until the writer inspects it. Ask them to check:

- whether first-person statements genuinely express their experience and judgment;
- whether Codex expansions, connections, and interpretations are visible and accurately labeled;
- whether anything important was omitted, over-organized, strengthened, or prematurely resolved;
- whether the recorded limits and possible directions still feel true at this reflective boundary.

The writer may accept the reflection unchanged, revise it directly, request revision, or reject it. Apply their corrections to the active record. Do not call the reflection writer-confirmed, use it as the writer's settled self-understanding, or carry its proposed direction into later work until this review gate closes.

## Work with content skills without becoming a hub

No content skill is required to produce a routine packet for meta-reflection. When the writer explicitly combines reflection with reading, thinking, writing, or speaking work, use the established content artifacts as evidence and keep the meta-level reflection distinct from the content result.

Do not turn the reflection record into a central task queue. Preserve a possible next direction only as the writer's choice, an unresolved option, or a clearly labeled model proposal.

## Return results

- For reflective dialogue, return the clearest present distinction or change and one consequential question when further reflection would help.
- For synthesis, return the writer's stated or previously confirmed changes, limits, method observations, and choices that matter at this boundary, while labeling consequential model organization or expansion for review.
- For preservation, return the saved record path, the categories actually preserved, and the points that need author review.
- State explicitly when no files were changed.

Do not add study advice, management actions, or future tasks merely because they seem generally useful.
