# Speaking Skill

## Purpose

Turn established material into speech that a real audience can follow and that the speaker can deliver naturally. Preserve the author's claims, uncertainty, examples, and voice while changing structure and wording where oral delivery requires it.

This is a lightweight speaking workflow. Do not create a production pipeline, media project, or presentation system unless the user separately asks for one.

## Establish the speaking situation

Infer from the request when possible, and otherwise establish only the missing details that materially change the result:

- source material or already chosen message;
- audience and their likely background;
- occasion and desired effect;
- target duration;
- language, tone, and delivery constraints.

Do not require a formal brief when the request and supplied material already answer these questions.

## Adapt for oral delivery

Build one audible through-line rather than reproducing the written document section by section.

- Make the opening establish the situation, question, or promise quickly.
- Prefer sentences that can be understood on first hearing.
- Make transitions and changes of direction audible.
- Retain necessary conceptual precision and qualifications.
- Use examples, repetition, signposting, and pauses only when they help this audience follow the thought.
- Estimate timing from the actual script or outline and identify sections that are likely to overrun.

Do not manufacture anecdotes, evidence, quotations, or personal experiences. Mark any missing factual support or author decision instead of smoothing over it.

## Choose the smallest useful output

Match the artifact to the request. Useful outputs include:

- a timed speaking outline;
- a full or partial oral script;
- cue cards or speaker prompts;
- a rehearsal plan with pacing and emphasis notes;
- likely audience questions and answer-preparation prompts;
- a delivery review of an existing script.

Do not automatically produce all formats.

## Preserve boundaries

For general learning, public expression, teaching, podcasts, or video narration, this skill may own the oral adaptation.

For an academic conference, defense, or research presentation, let `research-publishing` own the scholarly content, claim hierarchy, and medium-specific structure. Use this skill only when the user explicitly wants help with oral wording, timing, delivery, or rehearsal after that content is sufficiently stable.

Do not create or render slides. Hand visual production to the relevant presentation or PPT workflow without allowing that tool to change the argument.

Return unresolved source claims to the relevant reading workflow and unresolved substantive choices to the relevant thinking workflow.

## Return results

Return the requested speaking artifact first. Then add only the delivery information that helps the next use: estimated duration, deliberate omissions or compressions, passages needing author confirmation, and one practical rehearsal focus when relevant.
