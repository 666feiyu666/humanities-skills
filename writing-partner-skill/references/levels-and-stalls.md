# Levels, stalls, and jumps

## Four drafting levels

### Word

The writer cannot settle a term or phrase. Give a small set of candidates with what each buys (connotation, register, precision), or one recommendation with a reason. Do not rewrite the sentence unless the word choice forces it.

### Sentence

One sentence does not land or does not connect to its neighbors. Say what the sentence currently commits to, then offer one revision or one continuation. State what the revision changes. Keep the writer's claim and qualification intact; do not add facts or judgments the writer did not supply.

### Paragraph

A passage has a job in the argument: opening, claim, reason, example, objection, qualification, transition, close, or closing limits. In outline-led drafting, name it from the relevant strand of the line of argument. In prose-led revision, recover it from the realized passage and the writer's requested change, using the old line only for comparison. Draft the paragraph to do that job. Keep each paragraph to one job; when the job is unclear, ask rather than drafting. A `qualification` here is an intrinsic condition of the claim; defensive hedging and expository meta-discourse do not belong in the movement — route them to the closing limits section.

### Section

A section is one movement of a strand of the argument. Organize it into paragraphs by its ordered claims or questions, so the progression is inspectable. In outline-led drafting, draft it only after the writer confirms the strand and node; do not pad it or fill it with material the outline does not support. In prose-led revision, preserve the writer's intended scope and name any accepted structural divergence for later synchronization rather than forcing the section back into the old node.

## Stalls

Diagnose before drafting:

- **No word for it.** Supply candidates, not a paragraph.
- **Logical gap.** The step before does not license the step after. In outline-led drafting, if the line itself is missing the step, flag it and return it to `writing-skill`; do not invent the connection. In prose-led revision, ask whether the writer intends to add or remove a substantive step. Revise an intended change and name the divergence; return an unresolved choice to `writing-skill`. If the relation is already established and only the sentences are not joined, offer the connective or transition that states it.
- **Missing evidence.** The claim needs support the material does not yet give. Mark it as a source check for `reading-skill` and offer to draft around it with a placeholder.
- **Unclear job.** The writer does not know what this passage is for. In outline-led drafting, look up the job in the line; if none is assigned, return to `writing-skill` rather than inventing one. In prose-led revision, inspect what the passage actually does and the requested change; if its purpose still requires an unmade substantive choice, return that choice to `writing-skill`.
- **Perfectionism.** The writer will not accept any phrasing. Offer an explicitly provisional draft ("先写脏稿") to unblock, and make clear it is not settled prose.

Offer the smallest help that restarts drafting. One focused question beats a menu of options when the diagnosis is uncertain.

## Jumps

A jump skips a step or changes topic. Name it in chat:

- the reached point (C) and the skipped step (B);
- whether B belongs to the strand being drafted or to another strand or piece.

Ask the writer to supply B or to treat the jump as deliberate. In outline-led drafting, a deliberate argument change returns to `writing-skill`. In prose-led revision, an intended jump can be revised into the prose and named as a divergence for later synchronization; an unresolved jump returns to `writing-skill`. Here, never rewrite the line of argument.

## Voice

Draft in the target prose language. When the writer gives a writing sample, match its sentence length, openings, transitions, and punctuation. Leave deep de-AI editing to `humanizer`; this skill writes working prose, not the final polish.
