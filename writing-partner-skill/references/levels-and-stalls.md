# Levels, stalls, and jumps

## Four drafting levels

### Word

The writer cannot settle a term or phrase. Give a small set of candidates with what each buys (connotation, register, precision), or one recommendation with a reason. Do not rewrite the sentence unless the word choice forces it.

### Sentence

One sentence does not land or does not connect to its neighbors. Say what the sentence currently commits to, then offer one revision or one continuation. State what the revision changes. Keep the writer's claim and qualification intact; do not add facts or judgments the writer did not supply.

### Paragraph

A passage has a job in the argument: opening, claim, reason, example, objection, qualification, transition, or close. Name the job from the line of argument, then draft the paragraph to do that job. Keep each paragraph to one job; when the job is unclear, ask rather than drafting.

### Section

A section is one movement of the line of argument. Organize it into paragraphs by the ordered claims or questions of that movement, so the progression is inspectable. Draft the section only after the writer confirms the node; do not pad it to a length or fill it with material the outline does not support.

## Stalls

Diagnose before drafting:

- **No word for it.** Supply candidates, not a paragraph.
- **Logical gap.** The step before does not license the step after. Show the missing connection and offer a transition that states it, or flag it for the outline.
- **Missing evidence.** The claim needs support the material does not yet give. Mark it as a source check for `reading-skill` and offer to draft around it with a placeholder.
- **Unclear job.** The writer does not know what this passage is for. Return to the line of argument and name the job before writing.
- **Perfectionism.** The writer will not accept any phrasing. Offer an explicitly provisional draft ("先写脏稿") to unblock, and make clear it is not settled prose.

Offer the smallest help that restarts drafting. One focused question beats a menu of options when the diagnosis is uncertain.

## Jumps

A jump skips a step or changes topic. Name it in chat:

- the reached point (C) and the skipped step (B);
- whether B belongs to the current argument or to another piece.

Ask the writer to supply B or to treat the jump as deliberate. A deliberate change to the argument returns to `writing-skill`; here, do not rewrite the line of argument. Resume drafting from the corrected node.

## Voice

Draft in the target prose language. When the writer gives a writing sample, match its sentence length, openings, transitions, and punctuation. Leave deep de-AI editing to `humanizer`; this skill writes working prose, not the final polish.
