# Prose craft within the writing process

## Use with the active writing task

Use these shared criteria with the target-language quality reference during drafting, continuation, language review, and language revision. They connect sentence choices to the established argument, reader path, and voice. Keep checks internal unless a finding or author decision needs explanation. An outline-only task does not require a copyediting exercise; a small punctuation edit needs only the relevant criteria.

## Preserve meaning before improving form

Before revising a passage, identify what must survive: its distinct claims, examples, attribution, negation, comparison, quantity, conditions, time, completion status, and degree of certainty. Compare the result with the supplied passage or, for new prose, the writer-owned material and confirmed direction. A shorter sentence can still lose a claim; a more specific sentence can invent one.

In particular, do not turn a reported belief into the narrator's assertion, association into cause, a plan into an accomplishment, a lower bound into an exact number, or uncertainty into confidence. Changing punctuation can change which clause a qualification governs. Preserve the relation, not just the same nouns.

Use supplied particulars when available. When material is general, keep the expression appropriately general or identify the missing evidence outside the prose. Do not supply a scene, motive, statistic, or causal bridge merely to make the passage vivid. Voice samples supply evidence about expression, not facts to transplant.

## Build clear sentences and useful paragraphs

- Make the governing action and its participants easy to recover. Prefer a direct verb when a noun phrase hides the action. Keep passive constructions when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or less important than the affected object; do not invent an actor to satisfy an active-voice preference.
- Keep modifiers near what they modify and make the scope of negation and qualification clear. Distinguish an ambiguous grammatical relation from a complex but necessary idea.
- State the actual point directly when a defensive opening or negative contrast contributes nothing. Preserve negation and contrast when they establish a real distinction, limit, or disagreement.
- Give each paragraph a coherent job in the reader's movement. The point may emerge through an example or question; it need not appear in a compulsory first sentence or be repeated at the end.
- Use parallel construction when items have comparable functions. Break monotony when the form conceals differences in importance or relation. Neither symmetry nor constant variation is a goal in itself.
- Place emphasis where it helps the reader grasp this sentence or passage. Avoid burying the relevant action under a long preamble or adding an aphoristic closing line merely to sound decisive.
- Remove wording that does no necessary work. Retain explanation, purposeful recurrence, conceptual terminology, and qualifications the reader needs. Concision is not a demand for short sentences or reduced word count.

## Let punctuation express the relation

Choose sentence boundaries and punctuation after identifying what the clauses do: continue, distinguish, explain, insert, enumerate, qualify, or conclude. A comma, semicolon, colon, dash, and full stop are not interchangeable ways to vary appearance. Apply target-language syntax and the established editorial convention.

Repeated dashes, colons, parentheses, or fragments can indicate that the prose keeps postponing its main assertion or leaves relations unstated. Repair the sentence when that is the problem. Do not merely swap symbols, ban a punctuation mark, or enforce a frequency quota. Preserve meaningful pauses and asides in the author's voice. Keep source quotations and protected file content intact.

## Diagnose formulaic effects in context

A useful finding names the passage, the observable effect, and a proportionate repair. For example, explain that a concluding sentence repeats an already stated judgment, or that repeated contrasts introduce objections the reader has not encountered. Do not use an isolated word, punctuation mark, or three-item list as proof of AI authorship.

Look beyond vocabulary: repeated paragraph templates, unearned significance, obligatory summaries, decorative formatting, and conversational wrappers can flatten a passage even after stock words are replaced. Conversely, an ordinary connective or formal phrase may be doing necessary work. Leave an effective passage intact.

Natural prose need not become casual. Preserve the established register, technical level, and authorial temperament. Do not add slang, confession, anger, humor, intentional errors, or personal experience as evidence of humanity. Do not replace one uniform template with mandatory short sentences or a fixed number of examples.

## Check the affected whole and return the requested result

For a substantial passage, check the realized argument and reader path as directed in `SKILL.md`, then reread for sentence relations, punctuation, and cadence. These are connected checks within one task, not a required sequence of separately delivered drafts. Recheck meaning after language repairs, including any qualification moved by a split or merge. A narrow edit needs the surrounding context, not a fresh audit of the entire article.

In review mode, prioritize locations, effects, and suggested directions; do not automatically rewrite. In revision mode, return the revised scope and only the explanation needed for review. Do not append pattern inventories, self-scores, or claims that the prose passes an AI detector. Return consequential changes of claim or voice to author review under the existing gate.
