# Collaborative drafting and reviewable outlines

## Purpose

Help the writer move from a topic, rough outline, fragments, examples, partial judgments, or corrections into a structure that can support continuous prose. Treat this as writing work when the writer's direction exists but has not yet been arranged at article or section level.

This reference does not authorize Codex to discover the writer's governing question, choose among incompatible theses, or invent a design rationale. Those remain author decisions or `thinking-skill` work.

## Decide whether the outline should be visible

Return a visible detailed outline when:

- the user asks to review an outline before prose;
- a full article is requested from a rough outline or unordered material;
- a substantial new section or continuation introduces a new movement whose internal order is not yet settled;
- prose would otherwise conceal a consequential structural or ownership assumption.

Use an internal micro-outline and continue without interruption when the requested passage is short, its function is clear, and no consequential structural decision is exposed.

Skip a new outline when the user requests immediate drafting or has already confirmed an equivalent outline or writing brief.

## Reconstruct before elaborating

First preserve what the writer actually supplied:

- headings and hierarchy;
- sequence, including an intentionally provisional sequence;
- explicit questions and judgments;
- reasons, examples, experiences, quotations, and source notes;
- qualifications, doubts, parked branches, and requested omissions;
- wording that carries the writer's intended emphasis.

Do not improve the outline by replacing a specific reason with a familiar narrative. Do not treat a short heading as permission to infer a conventional claim.

## Elaborate the writing structure

For each substantial section, add only what helps the writer see how prose would proceed:

1. **Writing task**: what this section should let the reader understand, distinguish, reconsider, or carry forward.
2. **Ordered content**: the supplied points in a workable sequence.
3. **Available material**: the writer's examples, experiences, source claims, or quotations assigned to their likely role.
4. **Adjacent relation**: what the previous section established and why this section becomes relevant next.
5. **Open status**: any author decision, source check, or model-proposed substantive addition that remains outside the confirmed structure.

Use only as much detail as the scope needs. A detailed outline should make drafting inspectable, not simulate every future sentence.

## Separate structural elaboration from substantive addition

Codex may directly propose structural elaborations that preserve the writer's position, such as:

- dividing a broad heading into two writing tasks;
- ordering supplied reasons before examples;
- assigning one example to clarify a distinction;
- showing that a section first describes a process and then evaluates it;
- identifying the local conclusion needed before the next section;
- consolidating duplicated points.

Do not silently place these into the confirmed outline when they add substance:

- a new thesis, motive, reason, causal explanation, evaluation, or conclusion;
- a broader scope or stronger degree of certainty;
- an interpretation not supported by the writer's material;
- an objection or answer that changes the writer's position;
- a source-dependent claim that has not been established.

Put consequential additions under a short `Needs review` note or label them as model proposals. Ask for confirmation only when the proposal matters to the requested prose.

## Use local writing judgment

Three levels guide local decisions:

### Decide directly

Decide sentence and paragraph order, local emphasis, removal of repetition, explanatory depth for the named audience, and transitions entailed by established material.

### Propose or ask once

When two local arrangements or meanings are plausible and would produce materially different paragraphs, show one or two options or ask one focused question. State the consequence of the choice in plain language.

### Return to invention

Stop and return the exact issue to the writer or `thinking-skill` when the choice concerns the governing question, incompatible central claims, an overall argument path, or a substantive philosophical, moral, or practical commitment.

The existence of a gap does not determine the route; the consequence of deciding it does.

## Propagate writer corrections

Treat later writer explanations as authoritative updates, not isolated copy edits.

After a consequential correction:

1. state internally which earlier assumption it replaces;
2. identify every section, transition, example, and conclusion that depended on the old assumption;
3. update the detailed outline or prose consistently;
4. preserve any unaffected material;
5. do not repeat the rejected framing elsewhere under different wording.

## Compact outline form

Use a natural structure appropriate to the article. Render headings and review labels in the outline language. When explicit labels help review, use this compact form:

```markdown
## Section heading

Writing task: ...

- First movement using supplied material
- Second movement or example
- Local conclusion and relation to the next section

Needs review:
- Author decision, source check, or model proposal only if consequential
```

Do not require every label when ordinary headings and bullets already make the structure clear.

## Hand off to prose

When the writer confirms or corrects a visible outline, treat the accepted structure and corrections as the drafting authority. Do not reopen settled decisions merely to make the article more symmetrical or complete.

If the user asks for both an outline and immediate prose in one prompt, provide the outline first and then draft from it unless the outline exposes a consequential author decision that would make the prose misleading.
