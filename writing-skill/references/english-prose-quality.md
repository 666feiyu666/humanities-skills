# English prose quality

## Purpose

Use with [prose-craft.md](prose-craft.md) when drafting, continuing, reviewing, or revising English prose. Apply these language-specific criteria while composing as well as when editing. Review for fidelity, precision, idiomatic clarity, rhythm, and recurring formulaic tendencies often experienced as generic or model-like. Do not claim to detect whether text was written by AI. Diagnose observable language and rhetorical effects.

Accuracy of meaning comes before fluency. A natural sentence that changes the writer's reason, confidence, scope, conceptual distinction, or relation to a source is a failed revision.

## Review in this order

1. **Fidelity**: does the sentence express the writer's actual point and preserve ownership?
2. **Relation**: does the wording accurately express explanation, reason, cause, contrast, qualification, sequence, or conclusion?
3. **Reference and syntax**: are agents, pronouns, modifiers, negation, and sentence boundaries clear?
4. **Idiomatic clarity and rhythm**: does the prose read as situated English rather than a completed template?
5. **Compression**: can repeated work be removed without erasing a necessary distinction or transition?

Return to structure or the writer when no accurate local wording can repair the underlying idea.

When a sentence feels awkward and also carries several propositions, do not begin by swapping words or connectives. State the propositions and their relation in plain language, restore any missing middle step, and then write the sentence or paragraph again. Surface fluency cannot repair an unsettled inference.

## Detect generic abstraction

Flag language that sounds complete while leaving its object, agent, or reason unspecified:

- claims such as `a deeper understanding`, `the broader implications`, or `new possibilities` without naming what changed;
- generic motives substituted for the writer's stated reason or experience;
- nominalizations that hide who acts, decides, interprets, or changes;
- elevated verbs such as `delve into`, `underscore`, `illuminate`, or `serve as a testament to` used where a more exact action is available;
- a sentence that could move into many unrelated essays without alteration.

Repair by returning to the writer's concrete object, action, example, difficulty, or reason. Do not invent a scene or personal detail merely to make the prose feel human.

## Detect manufactured structure

Check whether apparent order exceeds the material actually established:

- automatic three-part enumeration or parallel headings whose contents operate at different levels;
- `not merely ... but ...` when the first term was never a live misunderstanding;
- `however`, `nevertheless`, `of course`, or `to be sure` without genuine pressure or contrast;
- `therefore`, `thus`, or `this means` where the warrant remains unstated;
- a symmetrical objection-and-response sequence that does not test the actual claim;
- a conclusion beginning with `ultimately` or `in conclusion` that announces more resolution than the body achieved.

Remove the signal, repair the relation, or mark the missing decision. Do not preserve balance or symmetry for its own sake.

Treat these as warning signs, not bans. Deliberate enumeration, rhetorical questions, direct address, visible transitions, pauses, and first-person metadiscourse can orient the reader and establish the writer-reader relationship. Preserve them when they perform that work, especially when the writer has chosen them. Ask whether the structure helps this reader follow this movement, not whether it resembles a generic list of prohibited forms.

## Detect repetitive model-like movement

Look across several paragraphs, not only one sentence:

- repeated openings with `Moreover`, `Furthermore`, `Importantly`, or another connective;
- repeated phrases such as `This highlights`, `This underscores`, `In this sense`, or `It is important to note` doing the work of unstated relations;
- every paragraph restating its point in a polished closing sentence;
- uniform topic-sentence, explanation, example, summary paragraph shapes;
- excessive balanced clauses, paired contrasts, or rhetorical triads;
- repeated metadiscourse announcing what the essay will do or has just done;
- planning language that describes why an example, question, or transition would be useful instead of actually using it;
- uniform sentence length and cadence;
- an outline or taxonomy copied into prose through one repeated grammatical frame for every item;
- several examples packed into one long sentence so that coverage replaces emphasis.

Vary form only after the underlying relations are clear. Naturalness does not require slang, fragments, contractions, or deliberate roughness.

When several parallel items are genuinely needed, first state why the set matters. Give the most important case room to develop, combine cases that perform the same work, and omit or subordinate secondary cases when the length constraint does not allow equal treatment. Do not vary sentence openings cosmetically while preserving the same mechanical paragraph underneath.

## Improve sentences and paragraphs

- Give important actions a visible agent when the argument depends on responsibility or causation.
- Give `this`, `it`, `they`, and abstract demonstratives a clear antecedent.
- Keep key terms stable; explain a deliberate change of meaning.
- Split a sentence when several relations cannot share one grammatical center.
- Combine short sentences only when their relation becomes clearer.
- Reduce modifier stacks and noun-heavy phrases when they delay the governing action.
- Use connectives when they state a real relation, not to manufacture flow.
- Let a paragraph have a recognizable nucleus without forcing claim-example-conclusion form.
- End sections with what has actually been established, not a generic uplift.
- Prefer one sentence with one governing grammatical and logical center. When a sentence must carry two relations, make their hierarchy unmistakable; when it carries three, split it unless the accumulation itself is purposeful and readable.

## English syntax and punctuation

- Keep the main subject and verb easy to locate; place a relative clause or modifier near its intended referent. An introductory participial phrase needs a recoverable grammatical attachment. If the responsible actor is not supplied, repair the construction without inventing one.
- Check agreement, pronoun reference, and tense against the actual time relation. A consistent present tense for discussing a book does not move historical events or earlier research into that same time frame.
- For ordinary expository prose, repair an accidental comma splice between independent clauses with a period, a semicolon, or an appropriate conjunction. Choose by relation and emphasis. Preserve a deliberate literary splice when it belongs to the writer's voice and does not confuse the reader.
- Distinguish restrictive from supplementary clauses. Commas can change which people or objects a statement applies to. Enclose a mid-sentence supplement with paired commas, parentheses, or dashes as appropriate; do not leave half of a pair after an edit.
- Use a colon after a grammatically complete lead-in to introduce its explanation or list in ordinary running prose. A semicolon can join related independent clauses or separate complex list items. Do not insert either simply to make sentences appear varied; headings and labels have different conventions.
- Preserve the established variety of English and house style for serial commas, quotation marks, possessives, and punctuation around quotations. Resolve ambiguity before enforcing optional preferences. Preserve the wording and meaning of source quotations and keep citation placement tied to the supported claim.
- Keep an em dash for a purposeful interruption or emphasis; rewrite when repeated dashes hide the underlying relation. Do not substitute hyphens for dashes or remove lexical hyphens from established terms in an attempt to erase AI-like features.
- Use grammatical parallelism for comparable list items and paired constructions. Repair a mismatch in form without making unlike claims seem equivalent. Retain deliberate fragments, questions, contractions, or long sentences when they suit this register and reader.

## Diagnose, repair, and preserve

These locally constructed examples illustrate contextual editing, not a substitute voice.

| Wording | Diagnosis and proportionate treatment |
| --- | --- |
| `The group is currently carrying out a comparison of the two accounts.` | When the noun frame adds nothing, write `The group is currently comparing the two accounts.` Preserve the ongoing action and both accounts. |
| `The notes were anonymous, we could not identify their authors.` | For ordinary exposition, a semicolon or period can clarify the clause boundary. Do not add a causal conjunction unless that relation is established. |
| `The manuscript—which survives in two copies—may be incomplete.` | The paired dashes hold a useful supplement. Keep them if they fit the voice, along with `may` and the number of copies. |
| `The interviews may suggest a change, highlighting a profound shift in society.` | The trailing phrase broadens and strengthens the initial possibility without support. Remove it if it adds no writer-owned claim; otherwise flag the change in scope. Do not replace it with an invented finding. |

Notice empty participial tails, stock praise, and generic statements of significance when they appear, but preserve a participial clause that supplies a supported action or relation. A watched word is a prompt to inspect meaning, not an automatic deletion.

## Preserve the writer's English

- Preserve the established variety of English, spelling convention, level of formality, and ordinary vocabulary unless the writer asks to change them.
- Retain first-person experience as experience; do not universalize it into a social law.
- Preserve uncertainty, hesitation, humor, and uneven emphasis when they are meaningful.
- Prefer the writer's supplied reason over a more elegant generic explanation.
- Do not make a non-native writer sound impersonally academic merely to remove every unusual but intelligible choice.
- Preserve quotations exactly as supplied and label any model translation.

Treat the writer's own revisions as higher-value voice evidence than generic requests such as `make it more natural`. Compare what changed: whether the writer made an agent more visible, replaced a defensive contrast with an affirmative statement, separated a hidden logical step, shortened an abstract sentence, introduced a direct question, or changed the balance of long and short sentences. Carry reliable patterns into later paragraphs while preserving genre and context.

Infer rhetorical habits as functions, not surface tokens. A writer's question may invite identification, a pause may keep the writer present, and explicit enumeration may reassure a reader facing unfamiliar material. Reproduce the relationship or movement when it recurs; do not mechanically repeat the same question form, filler phrase, or connective.

## Compact final pass

Before returning drafted or language-revised prose, check:

- Did any fluent sentence replace the writer's actual reason?
- Did any model proposal become an unmarked author judgment?
- Are the main pronouns, agents, and conceptual terms stable?
- Did quantities, conditions, attribution, time, completion status, negation, and uncertainty survive any split, merge, or deletion?
- Do punctuation and modifier placement preserve the intended scope and established English convention?
- Do connectives describe established relations?
- Are contrast, enumeration, and closure earned by the material?
- Do several paragraphs repeat the same movement or cadence?
- Did an outline, taxonomy, or source list survive as catalogue-like prose instead of a shaped paragraph?
- Did a planning note survive as prose that announces why an example or question is useful instead of enacting it?
- Did a rhetorical question get misread as a literal argumentative promise, or did a real argumentative question get treated as decoration?
- Can metadiscourse or nominalization be replaced by the actual action?
- Does the ending remain within the scope established by the body?

Report only consequential unresolved problems. Do not attach this checklist to ordinary output.
