# Processed thinking records

## Function

A processed thinking record preserves what happens when source material, experience, concepts, or an existing map enter the writer's own questions. It records a line of thought without converting it into an article.

Use this workflow when the writer requests a separate inquiry record. Keep ordinary inquiry, revision reasons, and alternatives in the conversation. An outline holds the current argument; do not expand it into a discussion record or automatically create another file. A requested record may remain exploratory and does not need a settled thesis.

Preservation does not bypass the argument-map editing gate. Before saving a new or revised map, including one embedded in a processed note, discuss the concrete plan in chat and obtain the writer's explicit confirmation. If that plan remains open, return the proposal and unresolved choices in the conversation instead. A closure or consolidation request alone does not confirm an unseen map plan.

Choose the record language from the writer's explicit request, the active record, or the local dialogue when no record exists. Render headings, field labels, relation labels, and status markers naturally in that language. Preserve quotations in their established wording and label any model translation. The English labels below name semantic roles rather than mandatory surface wording.

## Preserve ownership

Use localized equivalents of these labels only when they clarify provenance or status:

- `[source claim]`
- `[material]`
- `[writer question]`
- `[writer judgment]`
- `[writer reason]`
- `[personal experience]`
- `[model suggestion]`
- `[needs verification]`
- `[needs decision]`
- `[confirmed]`
- `[abandoned path]`
- `[scope decision]`

Do not make a tentative response more universal, confident, moralized, or theoretically complete than the writer has authorized. Never relabel a model proposal as an author judgment without confirmation.

## Choose the record variant

### Variant A: Exploratory thinking record

Use after heuristic dialogue that began without a clear map or direction.

Preserve:

1. the source, experience, phrase, image, discomfort, or blank state that triggered inquiry;
2. the writer's initial associations or lack of direction;
3. heuristic distinctions, comparisons, tensions, and questions introduced during dialogue;
4. emerging lines and the material each might organize;
5. the writer's responses to those lines;
6. selected, competing, rejected, and unresolved directions;
7. the point at which exploration stopped or transitioned into map refinement.

Suggested structure:

```markdown
# Conceptual title or provisional question

## Input and trigger

## What remains unclear

## Heuristic dialogue record

- [material / personal experience] …
- [model suggestion] One possible move is to distinguish A from B.
- [writer response] …

## Emerging directions

### Direction one

- Question it would answer:
- Material it could organize:
- Current status: [needs decision / confirmed / abandoned path]

## Writer decisions and scope

## Unresolved questions

## Relations to other notes
```

Do not disguise exploratory suggestions as an argument map. Several live directions may remain.

### Variant B: Map-refinement record

Use after reconstructing and questioning an existing text or visual thought map.

Preserve:

1. the original artifact and its location;
2. the faithful reconstruction;
3. ambiguous, unreadable, disconnected, or inferred relations;
4. each high-consequence structural diagnosis;
5. the writer's corrections and answers;
6. proposed revisions separately from the writer-confirmed map;
7. a decision ledger and unresolved source checks;
8. the scope at which the writer chose to stop.

Suggested structure:

```markdown
# Conceptual title

## Input and scope

## Faithful reconstruction of the original map

## Structural diagnosis

## Questions and corrections

## Writer-confirmed argument map

## Key relations

- A —supports / qualifies / rebuts / redefines→ B

## Writer decisions

## Basis and status

## Unresolved questions
```

In this requested record, preserve the original map and distinguish later corrections or superseded paths. Keep that history out of the active outline, which shows the current agreed argument.

### Variant C: Confirmed map and writing brief

Use only when the writer requests a separate record or brief for drafting and the writing-readiness conditions are met. An ordinary request to develop an outline does not require this variant. Follow `article-invention.md`.

## Line-of-thought memo

When useful, add a short memo explaining how one judgment led to another and what reasons prompted the change. It may use connected sentences, but stays with judgments and relations in the inquiry itself:

- explain the change in judgment;
- name the relation among nodes;
- preserve uncertainty and alternatives;
- avoid hooks, audience address, publication transitions, and rhetorical closure.

Do not infer from these changes what the writer has mastered, whether a learning method worked, or what they should do next. Those are matters for explicitly invoked meta-reflection, not this memo.

A separate working passage may test an opening, transition, or argument movement under writing-skill. Record what it tested and what changed in the outline. Use Humanizer (`humanizer` for English prose; `humanizer-zh` for Chinese prose) when the writer wants complete composition from the outline.

## Closure rules

- Return a requested summary or closing synthesis in chat. Save a separate record when the writer asks to save one; a pause or topic change alone does not require a file or a process section in the outline.
- Do not keep questioning merely to complete the template.
- In a writable project, use the project's processed-note location. Do not reconcile project or directory indexes as part of thinking work; treat any such maintenance as a separate writer-requested task, not as thinking closure or meta-reflection.
- Link backward to reading cards, maps, and source artifacts.
- Prefer a conceptual title; use a provisional question when no center has emerged.
- Preserve earlier versions when a consequential judgment changes.
- If no writable project exists, return the complete record as Markdown in the response.
