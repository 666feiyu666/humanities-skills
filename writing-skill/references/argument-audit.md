# Argument audit

## Purpose

Check what the article argues, how each claim is supported, how one step leads to the next, and whether a rebuttal identifies the real conditions and limits of a claim. Treat fluent prose as separate from a valid argument. Evaluate argumentative realization without deciding which substantive position the writer ought to hold.

## Reconstruct the whole article

State these items before judging individual sentences:

1. The motivating phenomenon or puzzle.
2. The central question.
3. The central claim in one or two sentences.
4. The sequence of numbered sections and the distinct job of each section.
5. The practical or philosophical consequence reached at the end.

Flag a title or conclusion that promises a different argument from the body.

## Audit each numbered section

Identify the section's rhetorical job and the six Toulmin elements where they are relevant. Do not force every element to appear as a separate sentence.

1. **Section question and job**: What part of the central question does this section answer?
2. **Claim**: What does the writer want the reader to accept, and which subclaims are required?
3. **Data or grounds**: What source, quotation, example, experience, distinction, or counterexample supports each claim?
4. **Warrant**: Why does the material support the claim? Write the missing bridge only when the writer's material already licenses it; otherwise flag it.
5. **Backing**: Why should the warrant itself be accepted in this field or context?
6. **Qualifier**: With what force, scope, population, time, or condition does the claim hold?
7. **Rebuttal**: What exception or strongest plausible objection limits the claim?
8. **Conclusion and transition**: What has now been established, and why does the next section follow?

Let one paragraph perform one recognizable role such as phenomenon, source claim, author claim, example, inference, objection, response, conclusion, or transition. Consolidate paragraphs that repeat the same role without advancing it.

## Audit rebuttal

For every consequential claim, identify the strongest plausible objection created by its wording and scope. Then check:

1. Does the essay answer that objection rather than a weaker substitute?
2. Does the objection reveal an exception, competing explanation, missing evidence, or conflicting value premise?
3. Should the response add backing, narrow the claim, change its qualifier, distinguish cases, or withdraw the inference?
4. Does a concession genuinely pressure the main claim, or merely make the writer sound balanced?

Do not require a separate counterargument paragraph when the claim is already carefully bounded. Do not insert rebuttal as a formula. Use it to test what the writer is entitled to claim.

## Keep claims and support distinct

- Mark the researcher's claim separately from a source's claim and a model suggestion.
- Treat personal experience as evidence of a possible experience, not automatic evidence of a universal social pattern.
- Support empirical claims about prevalence, institutions, history, or groups with a source or narrow their scope.
- Support conceptual claims through definitions, distinctions, examples, counterexamples, and explicit inference.
- Explain why an example instantiates the concept. Do not let an example stand in for the argument.
- Keep explanation separate from judgment. Showing how a phenomenon arises does not yet show whether it is good, harmful, or unjustified.
- Keep a social-theoretical lens from becoming the whole reality unless the argument establishes that scope.

## Test the path

Check whether:

- each section answers a question created by the previous section;
- the same key term retains one meaning, or a shift is explicitly explained;
- causal language has an identified mechanism;
- normative conclusions have a stated evaluative premise;
- a proposed alternative addresses the diagnosed problem rather than merely sounding preferable;
- the conclusion follows from the established claims and stays within their scope;
- the article preserves uncertainty where material remains provisional.

Use these markers for unresolved gaps:

- `[论点不清]`
- `[论证跳跃]`
- `[缺少材料]`
- `[需要来源]`
- `[范围过大]`
- `[重复论证]`
- `[与全文主张关系不明]`
- `[反驳未命中]`
- `[连接关系不成立]`

Do not silently invent the missing premise, evidence, source, or conclusion.

## Return a compact memo

After the prose, report:

- **中心论点**: one or two sentences;
- **论证路径**: one short line per numbered section;
- **论证缺口**: only material gaps, inferential jumps, or scope problems that remain;
- **长度检查**: the unit count and pass/fail result for every numbered section when mechanically verified.
- **循环移动**: whether the result should stay at macro, advance to relations, or return to invention.
