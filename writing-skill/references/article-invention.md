# Article invention

## Separate five layers

1. **Material**: passages, notes, cases, experiences, and concepts available.
2. **Puzzle**: what remains incomplete, contradictory, or unexpectedly connected.
3. **Candidate explanation**: a possible account of the puzzle.
4. **Judgment**: what the writer is prepared to affirm, criticize, qualify, or leave open.
5. **Writing claim**: what this piece may ask its reader to see differently.

Do not treat movement between these layers as automatic. Distinguish their roles during discussion; the outline should show the resulting argument without reproducing this taxonomy as required fields.

## Compare candidate centers

For each serious candidate, state:

- the central question and provisional claim;
- which notes, map branches, and evidence it organizes;
- the likely reader and genre when known;
- what it would leave outside or reserve for another piece;
- what source check or author decision it requires;
- how choosing it changes the argument path.

Do not declare one candidate philosophically or morally superior. Ask the writer to choose or keep alternatives alive.

## Confirm the argument path

Before making a writing brief, confirm:

- why the opening problem matters to the writer;
- what changed or complicated the writer's initial judgment;
- which claim is central and which claims are subordinate;
- why each major movement follows from the previous one;
- where evidence, warrants, qualifications, and objections attach;
- which ending follows from the established path;
- which next question belongs to a different piece.

Keep the current structure editable. When useful, test a relation or reader movement through a provisional passage and discuss its implications in chat. Present and confirm the concrete plan before creating or editing an argument-map or equivalent outline file, including an existing file that appears incomplete. Do not treat the trial as settled publication prose or a local answer as agreement to an unseen overall plan.

## Writing brief

Create a separate writing brief only when the writer requests one. Keep genre, reader, format, source-check, and drafting discussions in chat for an ordinary outline task. Do not expand the outline into the brief below. If a brief is requested, use only the fields that serve that request and render them in its language. The English labels below are optional semantic fields.

Create the brief only after the central direction is chosen or explicitly provisional. If candidates still compete, return conditional maps rather than a confident outline.

## Outline and composition

A clear outline showing claims and their logical connections is what the writer takes to writing-partner-skill for level-by-level composition, or to Humanizer for one-shot composition and language polish. Supply available material, constraints, voice evidence, and unresolved checks through the conversation; create a separate brief only on request. Exploratory sentences or passages can test the structure at any point. Level-by-level drafting from an established outline belongs to writing-partner-skill; one-shot composition and language editing belong to Humanizer (`humanizer` for English prose; `humanizer-zh` for Chinese prose). A user-supplied outline can go directly to writing-partner-skill or Humanizer without a writing-skill session.
