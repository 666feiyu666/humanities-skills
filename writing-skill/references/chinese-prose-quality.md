# Chinese prose quality

## Purpose

Use with [prose-craft.md](prose-craft.md) when drafting, continuing, reviewing, or revising Chinese prose. Apply these language-specific criteria while composing as well as when editing. Review for fidelity, clarity, naturalness, rhythm, and recurring formulaic tendencies often experienced as `AI味`. Do not claim to detect whether text was written by AI. Diagnose observable language and rhetorical effects.

Accuracy of meaning comes before fluency. A natural sentence that changes the writer's reason, confidence, scope, or conceptual distinction is a failed revision.

## Review in this order

1. **Fidelity**: does the sentence express the writer's actual point and preserve ownership?
2. **Relation**: does the wording accurately express explanation, reason, cause, contrast, qualification, sequence, or conclusion?
3. **Reference and syntax**: are subjects, pronouns, modifiers, negation, and punctuation clear?
4. **Naturalness and rhythm**: does the prose sound like situated writing rather than a completed template?
5. **Compression**: can repeated work be removed without erasing a necessary distinction or transition?

Return to structure or the writer when no accurate local wording can repair the underlying idea.

When a sentence feels awkward and also carries several propositions, do not begin by replacing words or connectives. State the propositions and their relation in plain language, restore any missing middle step, and then write the sentence or paragraph again. Surface smoothness cannot repair an unsettled inference.

## Detect generic abstraction

Flag language that sounds complete while leaving its object or reason unspecified:

- broad claims such as `真正的学习`, `更深层的意义`, or `新的可能性` without saying what changed;
- generic motives substituted for the writer's stated reason or line of thought;
- abstract nouns that hide actors and actions, such as repeated `进行……的构建`, `实现……的形成`, or `完成……的转化`;
- a sentence that could be moved into many unrelated essays without alteration.

Repair by returning to the writer's concrete object, action, example, difficulty, or reason. Do not add a vivid scene that the writer did not supply merely to make the paragraph feel human.

## Detect manufactured structure

Check whether apparent order exceeds the material actually established:

- an unsupported `首先／其次／最后` or automatic division into three points;
- `不是……而是……` when the first term was never a live misunderstanding;
- `当然／但是／然而` without genuine pressure or contrast;
- `因此／这意味着` where the warrant remains unstated;
- parallel headings whose contents operate at different conceptual levels;
- a conclusion that announces resolution, importance, or hope beyond what the body established.

Remove the signal, repair the relation, or mark the missing decision. Do not preserve symmetry for its own sake.

Treat these as warning signs, not bans. Deliberate `首先／其次／最后`, rhetorical questions, direct address, visible transitions, pauses, and first-person metadiscourse can orient the reader and establish the writer-reader relationship. Preserve them when they perform that work, especially when the writer has chosen them. Ask whether the structure helps this reader follow this movement, not whether it resembles a generic list of prohibited forms.

## Detect repetitive model-like movement

Look across several paragraphs, not only one sentence:

- identical paragraph shapes repeated in sequence;
- every paragraph opening with a connective or metadiscursive announcement;
- repeated phrases such as `也正是在这里`, `从这个意义上说`, `换言之`, or `这意味着` doing the work of unstated relations;
- several consecutive negative contrasts;
- uniform sentence length and cadence;
- a local summary after every small point;
- repeated explanation of what the article is about to do or has just done;
- planning language that describes why an example, question, or transition would be useful instead of actually using it;
- polished closing sentences that turn each paragraph into an aphorism;
- an outline or taxonomy copied into prose as repeated frames such as `以……为中心，可以……却……` for every item;
- several examples packed into one long sentence so that coverage replaces emphasis.

Vary form only after the underlying relations are clear. Naturalness does not require random sentence length, slang, fragments, or deliberate roughness.

When several parallel items are genuinely needed, first state why the set matters. Give the most important case room to develop, combine cases that perform the same work, and omit or subordinate secondary cases when the length constraint does not allow equal treatment. Do not vary sentence openings cosmetically while preserving the same mechanical paragraph underneath.

## Preserve the writer's presence

- Retain distinctive ordinary wording when it is accurate and readable.
- Preserve first-person experience as experience; do not universalize it into a social law.
- Preserve uncertainty, hesitation, humor, and uneven emphasis when they are meaningful.
- Prefer the writer's supplied reason over a more elegant generic explanation.
- Do not delete every repetition when recurrence records a real return, correction, or emphasis.
- Do not replace a concrete `我为什么开始在意这个问题` with impersonal process language unless the genre requires it.

Treat the writer's own revisions as higher-value voice evidence than generic instructions such as `更自然`. Compare what changed: whether the writer made the subject more visible, replaced a defensive contrast with an affirmative statement, separated a hidden logical step, shortened an abstract sentence, introduced a direct question, or changed the balance of long and short sentences. Carry reliable patterns into later paragraphs while preserving genre and context.

Infer rhetorical habits as functions, not surface tokens. A writer's use of a question may invite identification, a pause may keep the writer present, and explicit enumeration may reassure a reader facing unfamiliar material. Reproduce the relationship or movement when it recurs; do not mechanically repeat the same question form, filler word, or connective.

## Improve sentences and paragraphs

- Give important actions a visible subject.
- Keep key concepts stable; explain a change of meaning.
- Split a long sentence when it contains several relations that cannot share one grammatical center.
- Combine short sentences only when their relation becomes clearer.
- Use conjunctions when they state a real relation, not to manufacture flow.
- Let a paragraph have a recognizable nucleus, but do not force every paragraph into claim-example-conclusion form.
- Move between abstraction and concrete material at the pace required by the argument and genre.
- End sections with what has actually been established, not a generic uplift.
- Prefer one sentence with one governing grammatical and logical center. When a sentence must carry two relations, make their hierarchy unmistakable; when it carries three, split it unless the accumulation itself is purposeful and readable.

## Chinese syntax and punctuation

- Recover the topic and participants before adding subjects. Chinese can omit a recoverable subject or maintain a topic across clauses; an English-style requirement for an explicit subject in every sentence can make it less natural. Repair an unclear switch of actor or viewpoint instead.
- Unpack a stack of `的` modifiers when the reader cannot tell what modifies what. Prefer the actual action to redundant `进行／实现／构建` constructions, while preserving aspect and scope. Formal verbs, passive clauses, and four-character expressions may be appropriate to the text.
- Let `，` carry a continuing clause relation and `。` close a sufficiently complete movement. Split comma chains when the subject, level of claim, or qualification becomes hard to track. Chinese comma-linked clauses are not automatically English comma splices; do not impose an English clause rule on them.
- Use `、` for a simple series and `；` when parallel clauses or internally complex items need a clearer level of separation. Check whether the items really are parallel before regularizing their punctuation.
- Use `：` when the preceding wording calls for an explanation, quotation, or enumeration. Check where its scope ends. Repeated colon-led definitions may need a different sentence structure rather than another punctuation substitute.
- Keep `——` when an explanation, interruption, or turn of emphasis earns the pause; keep `（……）` for a supplement that can remain subordinate. Move a premise or essential qualification into the main syntax when an aside hides its role. Neither mark is prohibited by default.
- For newly composed Chinese prose, normally use Chinese full-width punctuation, `“……”` with `‘……’` nested, `《……》` for titles where appropriate, and `……` for an ellipsis. Follow an explicit house style or established `「……」／『……』` convention instead when applicable. Preserve quoted source wording, protected content, and existing valid project conventions; a style pass does not authorize changing citations or literal strings.
- Use question marks, exclamation marks, and ellipses for their actual function. An ellipsis that indicates a deliberate pause is different from an omission in a quotation; do not change a source quotation or its omission marks as a voice edit. Check paired marks and closing punctuation after splitting or merging sentences.

## Diagnose, repair, and preserve

These locally constructed examples illustrate decisions, not replacement templates. Apply them only when their problem is present.

| Wording | Diagnosis and proportionate treatment |
| --- | --- |
| `我正在对这三份访谈记录进行逐句的核对。` | If the extra noun frame adds nothing, write `我正在逐句核对这三份访谈记录。` Preserve the count and the ongoing action. |
| `这份材料至少保存了十二种称呼——其中三种只出现过一次。` | The dash introduces a useful detail. Keep it; retain `至少` and the relation between the two counts. |
| `研究者认为——这种关联可能存在——原因仍不清楚。` | If the asides obscure scope, write `研究者认为这种关联可能存在，但原因仍不清楚。` Do not turn the reported possibility into a finding of causation. |
| `这段批注不仅是一处标记，更是一扇通往理解的大门。它记录了我当时的疑问。` | When the opening metaphor adds no distinct judgment, the supplied point can stand as `这段批注记录了我当时的疑问。` Do not invent what the question was. If the writer intends the metaphor as a substantive evaluation, retain it or ask about that choice. |

Also inspect strings of praise after a factual clause, attribution such as `有人认为` that disappears during compression, unsupported stronger claims created by changing a verb, and chatbot greetings left inside an article. A greeting in a letter, a necessary attribution, or a purposeful recurrence may belong. Explain the actual mismatch rather than counting forbidden words.

## Apply personal or genre profiles carefully

General prose quality is not a single voice. A saved profile is evidence about a particular genre or project, not a universal rule for all Chinese writing.

Use [reflective-public-essay-profile.md](reflective-public-essay-profile.md) only when the user requests that voice or the active project establishes it. Do not apply its characteristic opening, conceptual movement, or ending to method explanations, design-process accounts, ordinary book reviews, or other genres merely because they are argumentative prose.

## Compact final pass

Before returning drafted or language-revised prose, check:

- Did any fluent sentence replace the writer's actual reason?
- Did any model proposal become an unmarked author judgment?
- Are the main pronouns and conceptual terms stable?
- Did counts, conditions, attribution, time, completion status, negation, and uncertainty survive any split, merge, or deletion?
- Do punctuation and modifier placement preserve the intended scope and established Chinese convention?
- Do connectives describe established relations?
- Are contrast, enumeration, and closure earned by the material?
- Do several paragraphs repeat the same movement or cadence?
- Did an outline, taxonomy, or source list survive as catalogue-like prose instead of a shaped paragraph?
- Did a planning note survive as prose that announces why an example or question is useful instead of enacting it?
- Did a rhetorical question get misread as a literal argumentative promise, or did a real argumentative question get treated as decoration?
- Can metadiscourse or nominalization be replaced by the actual action?
- Does the ending remain within the scope established by the body?

Report only consequential unresolved problems. Do not attach this checklist to ordinary output.
