# Discourse relations

## Purpose

Check whether sentences, paragraphs, and sections express the relation the argument actually establishes. Treat connectives as claims about thought, not decorative signals of fluency.

## Reconstruct the relation first

For each important transition, temporarily remove the connective and state the relation in plain language. Then decide whether the original wording is accurate.

Common relations include:

- **Elaboration**: the next unit explains or specifies the previous one.
- **Evidence**: the next unit gives material supporting a claim.
- **Warrant**: the next unit explains why material supports a claim.
- **Cause**: one event or mechanism produces another.
- **Reason**: a consideration justifies a belief, judgment, or action.
- **Result**: the next unit follows from an established cause or inference.
- **Contrast**: two comparable items differ along a shared dimension.
- **Concession**: a potentially opposing point is granted while the main claim still holds.
- **Condition**: the claim holds only under a named circumstance.
- **Counterexample**: a case limits or defeats a general claim.
- **Sequence**: the next unit follows in time or procedure, not necessarily in logic.
- **Reorientation**: the text changes the governing question or level of analysis.
- **Summary or closure**: the unit consolidates what has already been established.

## Detect common failures

- **False cause**: temporal order, association, or repeated co-occurrence is written as causation.
- **Missing warrant**: `因此` jumps from material to a conclusion whose bridge remains unstated.
- **Decorative contrast**: `但是` or `不过` introduces continuation, qualification, or a new topic rather than a genuine contrast.
- **Pseudo-concession**: `当然` names no real pressure on the main claim and only performs politeness.
- **Unequal comparison**: two terms contrasted by `一方面／另一方面` answer different questions or operate at different levels.
- **Hidden reorientation**: a paragraph changes from explanation to evaluation, or from individual experience to a social claim, without marking the shift.
- **Excessive chaining**: every paragraph begins with a connector, making the sequence sound logical without establishing its hierarchy.
- **Pronoun discontinuity**: `这`, `这种`, `它`, or `因此` has more than one plausible antecedent.

## Review at three scales

### Sentence

Check reference, subject, predicate, modifier scope, negation scope, and whether the connective matches the proposition on each side.

### Paragraph

Identify the paragraph's nucleus. Determine whether its other sentences supply background, explanation, evidence, qualification, objection, response, or closure. Flag sentences whose relation to the nucleus cannot be stated.

### Section and whole article

State why the next section is necessary after the previous one. Distinguish a logical consequence from a thematic association. A repeated topic word does not by itself create an argument path.

## Suggest repairs

Repair the underlying relation before replacing the connective. Depending on the problem:

- add the missing warrant;
- narrow the claim;
- reorder the units;
- split two relations now compressed into one sentence;
- replace a false causal statement with a descriptive association;
- mark a genuine change of question;
- remove the connective when juxtaposition already makes the relation clear.

Do not improve apparent flow by inserting transitions into an argument whose relation remains unsettled.
