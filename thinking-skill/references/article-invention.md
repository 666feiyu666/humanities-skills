# Article invention

## Separate five layers

1. **Material**: passages, notes, cases, experiences, and concepts available.
2. **Puzzle**: what remains incomplete, contradictory, or unexpectedly connected.
3. **Candidate explanation**: a possible account of the puzzle.
4. **Judgment**: what the writer is prepared to affirm, criticize, qualify, or leave open.
5. **Writing claim**: what this piece may ask its reader to see differently.

Do not treat movement between these layers as automatic. Preserve their roles in the argument map.

## Compare candidate centers

For each serious candidate, state:

- the central question and provisional claim;
- which notes, map branches, and evidence it organizes;
- the likely reader and genre when known;
- what it would leave outside or reserve for another piece;
- what source check or author decision it requires;
- how choosing it changes the argument path.

Do not declare one candidate philosophically or morally superior. Ask the writer to choose or keep alternatives alive.

## Confirm the argument path

Before making a writing brief, confirm:

- why the opening problem matters to the writer;
- what changed or complicated the writer's initial judgment;
- which claim is central and which claims are subordinate;
- why each major movement follows from the previous one;
- where evidence, warrants, qualifications, and objections attach;
- which ending follows from the established path;
- which next question belongs to a different piece.

Do not express this as publication prose. Keep it as an editable map and decision record.

## Writing brief

Render all headings and field labels in the brief language. The English labels below identify semantic fields rather than mandatory surface wording.

```markdown

## Writing situation

- Genre:
- Readers:
- Publication setting:
- Length or other constraints:

## Central question

## Writer-confirmed central judgment

- Degree of commitment:

## What this piece will not claim

## Confirmed argument map

## Relations between material and argument

## Provisional structure

## Checks to return to reading-skill

## Writer decisions still open

## Handoff to writing-skill

- Draftable scope:
- Qualifications that must remain:
- Decisions writing-skill must not make:
```

Create the brief only after the central direction is chosen or explicitly provisional. If candidates still compete, return conditional maps rather than a confident outline.

## Stop before prose

The brief is the terminal artifact of `thinking-skill`. Do not draft the title hook, opening paragraph, transitions, body paragraphs, or conclusion. Hand these tasks to `writing-skill`.
