# English collaboration for review suggestions

Use English for the review unless the writer explicitly chooses another collaboration language. Capability, evidence, and ownership rules remain in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Preserve the draft's and each source's language independently of the review language.

## Establish the basis without a questionnaire

Read the current draft or requested passage, including the ending when assessing its argumentative destination. Use the writer's stated genre, audience, discipline, and concerns; proceed when these are inferable instead of asking for a questionnaire. State a domain or audience assumption only when it affects the recommendations. When only an excerpt is available, do not claim to have assessed the entire piece.

## Shape the response in ordinary prose

Apply the point limit, ordering, and no-extra-sections rules in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). For each point, connect a located observation to its consequence and a concrete revision direction. For example, “The paragraph beginning ‘…’ treats two different claims as interchangeable. This leaves the reader unsure which claim the example supports. Distinguish them here before using the example.” This is a reasoning pattern, not a required sentence template or set of labels.

Keep each point compact; a short quotation or precise paragraph locator is usually enough. Mention a particular strength when it tells the writer what to preserve. Avoid stock verdicts such as “excellent overall, but room to improve,” inflated publication assurances, and unrequested numerical scores.

Return proposals, not the writer's settled conclusions. Do not edit or save files unless requested.
